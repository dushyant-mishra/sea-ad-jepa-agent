## Why this supersession is required

TD17 reported strong HVS↔SEA_AD relational geometry among shared native classes. TD18 extended a validation-only coarse crosswalk to NPH52. Those observations are retained, but they no longer support promotion of a shared biological scaffold.

Two independent TD19 attacks are decisive.

### 1. HVS native class is perfectly nested in operator/matrix

For the prospectively stated TD17 eligibility rule (>=30 Sample-B cells and >=20 Sample-B donors in each of HVS and SEA_AD), 19 classes qualify.

For **all 19/19** in HVS:
- operators per native class = 1
- matrices per native class = 1

The same SEA_AD classes span 8-10 operators.

Therefore HVS fine native-class identity is structurally confounded with operator/matrix. Cell-level donor×operator fixed-effect residualization removes essentially all HVS native-class centroid variation, so the biological-vs-operator contrast is not measurable at that resolution.

### 2. Depth-only pseudo-states reproduce scaffold-like geometry

Eight within-source source-library quantile bins, with no biological labels, produce HVS↔SEA_AD relational geometry r=0.980-0.986 across four deterministic common-scalar gene panels. The same attack restricted within the broad Glutamatergic and GABAergic classes still gives r=0.951-0.983.

Therefore high cross-source relational geometry is not discriminating evidence for biology.

### 3. Corrected TD17 roster provenance

TD17 SUMMARY.json said the eligibility rule yielded 19 classes, while TD17_SHARED_NATIVE_CLASS_ROSTER.csv contains 22 rows, including three classes that fail the stated threshold. The correct eligible 19 are preserved separately in TD19.

## What survives

- Relational state geometry remains an interesting hypothesis family.
- It is not qualified.
- TD17/TD18 may not be cited as evidence of a biological shared scaffold without a target construction that explicitly defeats acquisition/depth/operator geometry.
- The next candidate must be discovered within source under acquisition controls and may use cross-source data only as replication/generalization.
