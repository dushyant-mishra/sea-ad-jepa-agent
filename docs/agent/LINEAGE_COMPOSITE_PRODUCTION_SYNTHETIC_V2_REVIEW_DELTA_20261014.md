## Required correction 1 — explicit V79 + V80 combined production challenge inheritance

V2 currently inherits V79 sparse-positive stress but predates the completed V80 benchmark law. Production synthetic design must now distinguish:
- broad V79-style hierarchical/observation challenge geometry; and
- V80-style sparse-positive estimability/abstention geometry.

The production generator may reuse the *principles* and challenge classes, but must not copy frozen V80 seeds, thresholds or benchmark identities into production worlds. V79 remains historically `NOT_QUALIFIED`; V80 remains a separately frozen estimator-independent benchmark.

Production sparse-positive controller truth must distinguish at least:
- estimable sparse-positive cases;
- genuinely non-estimable-by-construction cases;
- numerical algorithm failure as a downstream estimator outcome, never as a truth class.

Where an estimator-facing qualification is later authorized, the status semantics must remain compatible with `COMPLETE`, `NOT_ESTIMABLE`, `NUMERICAL_FAILURE`, and false abstention / false certainty must remain hard errors.

## Required correction 2 — empirical real-distribution calibration must be explicit but non-circular

V2 correctly says empirical QC/depth/support distributions from V72-V75 are consumed by observers, but the production contract should explicitly bind the provenance of those calibration summaries.

Required law:
- observation-side calibration inputs must come from authenticated real TRAIN/S174 or later repaired authority artifacts;
- their hashes/provenance and scope must be frozen before sealed-world outcomes are inspected;
- they may govern realistic depth, support, dropout/detection, source composition, modality availability and related acquisition envelopes;
- they may not be promoted into human biological effect-size truth;
- frozen V79/V80 realized parameters, failed fit IDs or outcome-driven retuning may not be used to tune production worlds;
- stress worlds may deliberately extend beyond empirical prevalence ranges, but such extensions must be labeled synthetic stress rather than empirical calibration.

This keeps the simulator distribution-informed without turning the benchmark into a numerical copy of the human data.

## Required correction 3 — evidence-response and depth-response curves

V2 separates biological and measurement uncertainty conceptually, but does not require the strongest diagnostic introduced by the architecture work.

For selected cells/query cases, production truth must support two distinct intervention families:

1. **Biological-evidence response** — hold the underlying cell and observation operator law fixed while varying the amount of biologically relevant evidence available to the student (for example 20/40/60/80/100%). Measure convergence of inferred state/query target toward the full-evidence reference.
2. **Measurement-depth response** — hold hidden biology fixed while varying sequencing/measurement depth or repeated observation quality. Measure convergence attributable only to observing the same biology better.

These two curves must be separately scored so increased biological information cannot be confused with improved measurement of unchanged information.

## Required correction 4 — held-out measurement-domain transfer and biological-novelty separation

V2 includes held-out donors and source/operator stress, but it does not explicitly require the final synthetic ecosystem to separate:
- unfamiliar measurement/acquisition domain;
- familiar measurement domain with biologically novel state;
- both simultaneously.

Production qualification should therefore include sealed transfer cases across at least donor, source/dataset and operator/technology boundaries where supported by the calibrated observation model.

Controller truth must label biological novelty separately from measurement-domain OOD. A representation should not be penalized for retaining real biological novelty merely because a technology classifier can distinguish measurement domains; conversely, technology shift must not be mistaken for biological novelty.
